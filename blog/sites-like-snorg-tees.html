Sites like Snorg Tees: 7 funny & weird graphic tee brands worth your money

July 29, 2026·10 min read

If you typed "sites like Snorg Tees" into Google, you already know the feeling: you remember a place that sold funny, slightly-too-clever graphic tees, you went back to buy another, and the magic wasn't quite there anymore. So you're shopping for a replacement. Good news — the weird-tee corner of the internet is alive and well. You just have to know where it moved.

Here's an honest rundown of where funny and weird graphic tees actually live in 2026, with the trade-offs on each, and no pretending every option is equally good.

Wait — what happened to Snorg Tees?

Snorg Tees was one of the defining funny-t-shirt sites of the late 2000s and early 2010s — pun shirts, nerd humor, one-liner graphics, and an advertising budget that seemed to cover every corner of the early internet. It came up in the same breath as Busted Tees, its close cousin. For a while, if you wanted a shirt that made a joke, this was a first stop.

The genre didn't die — it scattered. Print-on-demand made it possible for tiny independent brands to make one great shirt at a time instead of mass-printing a catalog of thousands. The result is that the funny tee is healthier than ever, but the good stuff is spread across smaller, weirder shops instead of one giant billboard of a website. That's what you're actually looking for when you search "sites like Snorg Tees." So here they are.

1. Becky Wexlin (that's us — start here if you like it strange)

We're going to put ourselves first because we built this list, and also because if your favorite thing about the old funny-tee sites was the shirts that were genuinely weird — not just a pun, but a whole strange point of view — this is the lane we live in.

The catalog runs from deadpan one-liners like the Fuck You For Shopping tee to full-on absurdism like Delusional But Thriving, meme relics like the Dicks Out for Harambe shirt, and pun-shaped nonsense like the T-Rexcellent tee. Everything's printed on demand on premium blanks — Comfort Colors, Bella+Canvas, AS Colour — so the shirt survives more than a few washes.

The case for it: original designs with an actual voice, heavier blanks than the fast-fashion funny-tee sites, and a human answering emails. The catch: we're small, so the catalog is curated rather than infinite — if you want ten thousand generic options, that's not us. If you want a few dozen shirts that are genuinely worth wearing, browse the funny t-shirts or the full weird graphic tee collection.

2. Threadless

The original artist-community tee site, and still one of the best. Designs are submitted and voted on by a huge community of illustrators, so the range is enormous and the art is often genuinely good.

The case for it: massive, ever-changing catalog with real artistic range. The catch: because it's a marketplace, quality and print method vary from design to design, and the sheer volume means you'll do a lot of scrolling to find the gems.

3. Busted Tees

Snorg Tees' closest sibling from the same era, and still around. If you specifically miss the mid-2000s pop-culture-reference-and-pun house style, this is the most direct nostalgia hit on the list.

The case for it: it's the closest thing to "Snorg Tees but it still exists." The catch: the humor can feel frozen in a specific internet era, which is either exactly what you want or exactly what you don't.

4. TeePublic

A giant independent-artist marketplace (owned by Redbubble) with a strong search and an endless supply of fandom, meme, and niche-interest designs. If you have a very specific reference in mind — an obscure show, a deep-cut meme — someone on TeePublic has probably made a shirt for it.

The case for it: unbeatable for niche and fandom-specific designs. The catch: it's a print-on-demand marketplace at massive scale, so blanks tend toward the lightweight end and you're buying the design more than the garment.

5. 6 Dollar Shirts

The budget option. Simple, text-forward funny shirts at a price that made the name. If you want a joke on a shirt and you do not want to think about fabric weight, this is the pragmatic pick.

The case for it: cheap, cheerful, and honest about what it is. The catch: you get what you pay for on the blank and the print — these are disposable-fun shirts, not heirloom pieces.

6. Redbubble

The other giant artist marketplace. Same basic idea as TeePublic — endless independent designs across every conceivable niche — with the same trade-offs. Good for finding something extremely specific; less good if you care a lot about the shirt itself.

The case for it: enormous selection, strong for niche interests and fan art. The catch: quality is design-dependent and blanks are basic.

7. Your local weird brand

Not a website — a category. The best replacement for Snorg Tees is often a small independent brand run by one or two people with a distinct sense of humor, the kind of shop you find through a friend, a TikTok, or an Etsy rabbit hole. (We started on Etsy too.) These brands can't compete on catalog size, but they win on originality, and they're the ones actually pushing the weird-tee form forward.

What to actually look for in a Snorg Tees alternative

Whatever site you land on, three things separate a shirt you'll wear for years from one you'll wear twice:

The blank matters more than you think. A brilliant design on a see-through 4-ounce shirt is still a see-through 4-ounce shirt after two washes. Look for brands that name their blanks — Comfort Colors, Bella+Canvas, AS Colour, Next Level. Silence about the fabric usually means it isn't a number they're proud of. We broke this down in what makes a quality graphic tee.

Original art beats recycled memes. Anyone can slap a viral image on a shirt. The designs worth owning have a point of view — a joke that's actually written, not just referenced. That's the whole difference between a shirt that's funny for a week and one that's funny for years, which we get into in why meme shirts are having a moment.

Price honesty. A good independent graphic tee runs roughly $25–$45, and that's not a markup — it's the real cost of a heavier blank, on-demand printing, and a small operation. If a shirt is eight dollars, the savings came out of the fabric. We did the full math in why graphic tees are so expensive now.

The bottom line

Snorg Tees walked so a thousand tiny weird brands could run. The funny graphic tee didn't go anywhere — it just got better, stranger, and more spread out. Whichever site you pick from this list, you're in better shape than you were with one giant catalog of the same twenty jokes.

And if the thing you actually miss is the weird — the shirts that make a stranger stop and squint — you know where we are.

The funny tee isn't dead. It just moved somewhere weirder, and put on a heavier blank.
